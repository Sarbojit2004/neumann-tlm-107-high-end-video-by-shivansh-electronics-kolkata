**Language:** English only

## Tone: Precise & Technical-but-Accessible, opening and closing warm

The creative brief's Section 9 offers three personas. This script uses the
**second — Precise and Technical-but-Accessible**, the voice of a confident,
spec-literate working engineer — as its spine, with the **Warm and Trustworthy**
persona's consultative register carrying the opening problem and the closing
commercial block.

This is the same blend the companion reel uses, deliberately: the two
deliverables should sound like one campaign. What changes is **depth, not
register**. The reel had 88 seconds and could only assert that five patterns
matter. This has 298, so each pattern gets explained for what it actually
does in a session, the transformerless circuit gets a real sentence rather
than a label, and the 15-second LED auto-dim — a genuinely thoughtful piece of
industrial design — gets its own moment instead of being cut.

**Delivery notes.** Target **145–155 wpm** — very slightly slower than the reel,
because the technical passages need room. Land hard on the numbers (*141*,
*10*, *five*) and give each a beat. The five-pattern walk in Chapter 2 is five
distinct thoughts: pause between them rather than running them together. Do not
lift at the end of the price line — state it as a fact and let it sit.

### CHAPTER 1 · 0:00 – 0:25 · The versatility problem  *(25 s · 60 words)*

> Every studio runs into the same physics problem.
> *[pause 0.8s]*
> Different sources want different polar responses — and a microphone with a
> fixed pattern has already made that choice for you.
> *[pause 0.6s]*
> Too much room on an intimate take. Too much bleed when two players face each
> other. Too much proximity effect up close.
> *[pause 0.5s]*
> The usual answer is to own more microphones. There is a better one.

---

### CHAPTER 2 · 0:25 – 1:15 · The capsule and the five patterns  *(50 s · 128 words)*

> Inside the Neumann TLM 107 is a purpose-built, edge-terminated dual-diaphragm
> capsule. Two diaphragms, both resting at ground voltage — and balancing them
> against each other is what produces every pattern the microphone offers.
> *[pause 0.7s]*
> There are five, and they are all selected from one toggle on the rear.
> *[pause 0.8s]*
> **Omnidirectional** — no proximity effect at all, and the flattest low end of
> the five. For a room worth hearing, or a performer who moves.
> *[pause 0.4s]*
> **Wide angle cardioid**, when a voice needs to sit in its space rather than
> in front of it.
> *[pause 0.4s]*
> **Cardioid** — the everyday workhorse. It rejects what is behind it.
> *[pause 0.4s]*
> **Hypercardioid**, the tightest forward lobe, where bleed between two close
> sources has to go.
> *[pause 0.4s]*
> And **figure-8** — equal front and rear, dead to the sides. Two voices across
> a table, or the side channel of a Mid/Side pair.
> *[pause 0.6s]*
> The response stays balanced across all five. So you change how much room you
> capture without ever changing how the source itself sounds.

---

### CHAPTER 3 · 1:15 – 2:00 · Engineering and the interface  *(45 s · 116 words)*

> The circuit behind that capsule is transformerless. An electronic stage
> replaces the output transformer entirely, and that is what buys the
> exceptionally large dynamic range, the linearity across the whole span, and
> the interference rejection a balanced output needs.
> *[pause 0.7s]*
> It also means one microphone covers both extremes. A maximum sound pressure
> level of a hundred and forty-one decibels — a hundred and fifty-three with
> the pad engaged — against a self-noise floor of just ten dB-A.
> *[pause 0.8s]*
> A whispered take and a snare at full force are the same instrument's problem
> here.
> *[pause 0.5s]*
> Pattern, pre-attenuation and low-cut all live under a single rear navigation
> toggle, with an illuminated ring showing the current state.
> *[pause 0.5s]*
> And after fifteen seconds, that illumination fades out on its own — so
> nothing glows at a performer in a darkened room while they work.

## Constraints this script observes

- **No captions.** These words are never rendered as on-screen typography. The
  video's on-screen text is an independent hierarchy; nothing here is burned in.
- **No competing brand** is named — not another manufacturer, and not another
  microphone in Neumann's own catalogue. The M 49 appears once, as the
  documented design lineage of this instrument's headgrille, which is a
  statement about its own ancestry rather than a comparison with a product a
  viewer could buy instead.
- **No distributor, dealer or reseller language.** Shivansh Electronics is
  named only as Neumann's Authorized Partner.
- **No wooden box** and no packaging language of any kind — not even the idiom
  "in the box". The Studio Set's confirmed contents are the microphone and the
  EA 4 shockmount; the brief's Section 4 marks the enclosure UNVERIFIED.
- **One price, stated once**, at 4:20 — Rs. 1,44,900, always with the
  inclusive-of-GST qualifier. No rounding, no "starting from", no alternative
  figure, no urgency or discount framing.
- **Every technical figure is VERIFIED** in the brief's Section 4 master table:
  five polar patterns, 141 dB maximum SPL, 153 dB with the −12 dB pad, 10 dB-A
  self-noise, 20 Hz–20 kHz, edge-terminated dual-diaphragm capsule,
  transformerless circuitry, the 15-second display timeout, the EA 4
  shockmount, and the matte nickel and black finishes.
- **www.shivanshelectronics.in is the spoken destination**, matching the video's
  on-screen emphasis, and it is the last thing said. The full product-page URL
  is shown on screen in the outro but is never read aloud — it is not something
  a listener can retain.
- **Three workflows, not eleven.** The brief's supporting research documents
  eleven professional workflows. Chapter 6 takes the three that speak to the
  buyer profile the brief identifies and gives them real specificity, rather
  than skimming all eleven and turning the video into a manual.

## Recording notes

Replace the silent placeholder at `public/vo/voiceover-longform.mp3` with the
recorded take, then re-render:

```bash
npm run render:lf
```

The take should be **298.000 s or shorter**. The composition is a fixed 8,940
frames and the voiceover track is placed at frame 0, so a longer file is
truncated rather than extending the video. If a take runs long, trim the marked
pauses first — they are the intended slack, and Chapter 2's inter-pattern
pauses are the most compressible.
